Content Moderation Guidelines

Policy Classification and Severity Assessment

Project: AI Safety and Content Moderation
Author: Rupech Nkengafack

1. Purpose

These guidelines establish a consistent framework for reviewing user-generated content, identifying potential policy violations and determining appropriate moderation actions.

The objective is to distinguish genuinely harmful content from harmless discussion, criticism, educational material and expressions of personal opinion.

2. Moderation Categories

Harassment

Targeted abusive language intended to insult, intimidate, humiliate or repeatedly attack another individual.

Hate Speech

Content that attacks, dehumanises or promotes discrimination against people based on protected characteristics.

Threats

Statements expressing an intention to cause physical harm, intimidate someone or encourage violence against an identifiable target.

Self-Harm

Content expressing personal self-harm intentions, encouraging self-injury or describing relevant experiences.

Educational, research and supportive discussions should not automatically be treated as violations.

Sexual Content

Explicit sexual material, sexual coercion or inappropriate sexual requests.

Context, consent and the nature of the material must be considered.

Violence

Content promoting, threatening or encouraging physical harm. Historical, fictional, journalistic and educational discussions of violence are not automatically violations.

Spam

Repetitive, deceptive or unsolicited promotional material, including suspicious attempts to obtain sensitive information.

Safe Content

Content that does not violate the project’s moderation rules, including criticism, disagreement, educational discussion and ordinary personal expression.

3. Severity Classification

Severity	Definition	General Response
Low	Minor concern with limited potential harm.	Review or allow with caution.
Medium	Clear policy concern requiring moderation.	Remove or restrict according to context.
High	Serious harmful content or credible risk.	Remove and escalate where appropriate.
Critical	Immediate or potentially severe safety concern.	Urgent escalation.

Safe content receives no severity rating.

4. Moderation Decisions

The following decisions are used:

* Allow: Content is acceptable under the project’s rules.
* Allow with caution: Content is borderline and may require monitoring or additional context.
* Remove: Content violates the applicable moderation rule.
* Escalate: Content requires specialist review or urgent safety handling.

Removal and escalation are not interchangeable. Some content may require both actions.

5. Contextual Assessment

Before making a decision, consider:

1. Who or what is being discussed?
2. Is the content directed at a specific person or group?
3. Does it express an intention to cause harm?
4. Is the content educational, fictional, journalistic or academic?
5. Is the language literal, figurative or sarcastic?
6. Could the content indicate immediate danger?
7. Does the message request, encourage or facilitate harmful behaviour?

6. Important Annotation Principles

Do not confuse criticism with harassment.

A customer saying that a company provides terrible service is expressing dissatisfaction, not necessarily targeting an individual.

Do not confuse discussion with endorsement.

Discussing historical violence or researching self-harm does not automatically indicate harmful intent.

Assess threats carefully.

Threatening language should be reviewed according to its target, specificity, context and apparent seriousness.

Prioritise immediate safety concerns.

Expressions of imminent self-harm require urgent safety-oriented handling rather than a simple content-removal decision.

Avoid unsupported assumptions.

When context is insufficient, flag the case for further review instead of inventing intent.

7. Quality Assurance Checklist

Before finalising a moderation decision, verify:

* The content was interpreted in context.
* The correct category was selected.
* Severity reflects the potential harm.
* The moderation action is appropriate.
* The rationale explains the decision.
* Harmless discussion has not been incorrectly flagged.
* Urgent safety concerns have been identified.

8. Limitations

This is a simplified, independently developed moderation framework for portfolio demonstration.

It is not an official platform policy. Real moderation environments require platform-specific rules, escalation procedures and specialist safety protocols.
