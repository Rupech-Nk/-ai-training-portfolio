Sentiment Annotation Guidelines

Project

Context-Aware Customer Support Sentiment Analysis

1. Purpose

These guidelines establish a consistent process for classifying customer-support messages according to their expressed sentiment.

2. Available Labels

POSITIVE

* Customer expresses satisfaction.
* Customer appreciates assistance.
* Customer describes a favourable experience.
* Customer recommends a service.

NEGATIVE

* Customer expresses frustration or dissatisfaction.
* Customer reports poor service.
* Customer describes an unresolved problem.
* Customer communicates an unfavourable experience.

NEUTRAL

* Customer provides factual information.
* Customer asks a straightforward question.
* Customer requests assistance without expressing clear emotion.
* Customer provides an update without evaluating the experience.

3. Annotation Procedure

1. Read the entire message.
2. Identify the customer’s overall emotional position.
3. Consider the context and intended meaning.
4. Select the most appropriate sentiment label.
5. Write a brief explanation supporting the decision.
6. Assign a confidence rating.

4. Confidence Levels

Level	Description
High	Clear sentiment with strong supporting evidence.
Medium	Sentiment is identifiable but contains some ambiguity.
Low	Insufficient evidence or competing interpretations.

5. Special Cases

Mixed sentiment: Consider the overall message and identify which sentiment is dominant.

Sarcasm: Interpret the intended meaning rather than relying on literal wording.

Unresolved complaints: Classify according to the customer’s expressed dissatisfaction.

Information requests: Do not assume a negative sentiment simply because the customer needs assistance.

Unclear messages: Flag for further review rather than making unsupported assumptions.

6. Quality Standards

All annotations must:

* Follow the approved label definitions.
* Be supported by the message context.
* Include a clear rationale.
* Use confidence ratings consistently.
* Avoid assumptions about the customer’s emotional state.

7. Escalation

Messages that cannot be confidently classified should be flagged for secondary review and possible adjudication.

8. Documentation

Every annotation should retain its original message, assigned label, rationale and confidence rating.

Document type: Independent portfolio simulation.
